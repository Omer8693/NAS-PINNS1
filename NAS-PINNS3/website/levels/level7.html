Level 7

Multi-Domain NAS — Poisson Equation

NAS is applied independently to five different 2-D geometries, all solving the Poisson equation. This tests whether NAS-PINNs generalise across domain shapes — from simple circles to complex 5-petal flower boundaries.

5 geometries Poisson PDE: −∇²u = f Circle L2 = 0.00014 ✓✓ Bayesian · NSGA-II · NSGA-III
Problem Setup

Poisson Equation on 5 Geometries

PDE

−∇²u = f(x, y) on Ω
u = 0 on ∂Ω (Dirichlet)

Each domain has a different exact or reference solution. The PINN is trained using only the PDE loss — no labelled solution data. Accuracy is measured as L2 relative error against the reference.

Why Different Geometries?

Geometry complexity tests the PINN's ability to learn complex boundary shapes. Re-entrant corners (L-Shape) and curved petals (Flower) create challenging boundary conditions and singular features in the solution.

Takeaways

Key Observations

  • ✓
    NAS generalises across PDE families and geometries Circle L2=0.00014, Annulus L2=0.009, L-Shape L2=0.028 — four of five geometries solved to engineering accuracy.
  • ✓
    Smooth boundaries are dramatically easier Circle (smooth, convex) achieves 3 orders of magnitude better accuracy than Flower (complex curved boundary).
  • →
    Flower domain requires architecture-level adaptation L2=0.172 for all three optimisers suggests the search space (standard MLP) cannot easily capture the high-frequency boundary features of the 5-petal shape.
  • ✓
    Bayesian NAS consistently finds the best architecture Bayesian wins on all 4 evaluated domains. NSGA-II is competitive on Circle and L-Shape but falls behind on complex geometries.
