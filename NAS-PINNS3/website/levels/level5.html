Level 5 of 7
Extended Training

The best NAS-found architectures from Level 1 are retrained with a longer cosine learning-rate schedule — no architecture changes, only more training time. NSGA-II improves 4.5× and finally meets the L2 < 0.10 target. Note: L-BFGS was not used (lbfgs_iters=0).

Same architectures as L1 Cosine LR decay · Adam only L-BFGS: not used (lbfgs_iters=0)
Training Setup

Extended Training Configuration

Level 5 answers a key question: were the Level 1 architectures fundamentally limited, or just under-trained? The answer — confirmed here — is that epoch count was the bottleneck, not architecture capacity.

L-BFGS Note

Second-order L-BFGS optimisation was planned but not executed (lbfgs_iters=0 in the configuration file). All improvements in Level 5 come exclusively from extended Adam training with cosine LR. L-BFGS might further improve convergence speed but is untested here.

LR Schedule Equation
LR(e) = LR_min + ½(LR_max − LR_min)(1 + cos(π·e/N))

LR_max = 1×10³, LR_min = 1×10&sup6;, N = total epochs

The cosine learning-rate schedule starts high (1×10³) to explore the loss landscape broadly, then anneals smoothly to a very small value (1×10&sup6;) to refine the solution precisely. Compared to a fixed or step-decay schedule, cosine annealing avoids the abrupt transitions that can destabilise PINN training at high-frequency loss components. The extended training budget (~50,000 epochs for L5 vs ~20,000 for L1) provides a 2.5× deeper optimisation for all three architectures, revealing how much of the L1 gap was due to insufficient training rather than architecture limitations.

The largest improvement is seen for NSGA-II, which improves 4.5× in L2 (0.252 → 0.055) and 4.6× in MAE (132.6 → 28.6°C). This dramatic gain shows that the 3×153 tanh architecture has sufficient capacity for the problem — it just needed more training epochs to converge. Bayesian improves 2.6× to reach L2=0.030, which is 3× below the production target. NSGA-III improves 2.0× but only reaches L2=0.259 — still more than 2.5× above the target. The plateau in NSGA-III improvement is evidence that its 12k-parameter architecture is genuinely capacity-limited, not just under-trained.

L1 vs L5 L2 Error — All Optimisers

The grouped bar chart shows L1 (light) and L5 (coloured) L2 errors side by side for all three optimisers. The dramatic height reduction from L1 to L5 for NSGA-II is the most visually striking result: the L5 bar drops below the target line (green dashed at 0.10) while the L1 bar was well above it. Bayesian's already-low L1 bar shrinks further to 0.030. NSGA-III's modest improvement — and the L5 bar remaining well above the target — provides visual confirmation that this architecture has reached its capacity ceiling.

L2 Improvement — L1 vs L5

This before/after L2 comparison from the results directory shows paired bars for each optimiser: the left bar of each pair is L1 (short training), the right is L5 (extended). The green shading or line marks the L2=0.10 target. The key story is the NSGA-II pair: L1 was more than 2× above target, but L5 drops to 0.055 — clearly below the target. This plot illustrates the main message of Level 5: training budget, not architecture, was the primary bottleneck for two of the three NAS optimisers.

L2 vs Training Epoch

The L2-vs-epoch training curve shows the convergence behaviour for all three architectures during Level 5 extended training. Bayesian (blue) converges rapidly in the first ~10,000 epochs then asymptotes smoothly. NSGA-II (orange) shows steeper early improvement but continues gaining even at 40,000+ epochs — consistent with its large 4.5× improvement. NSGA-III (purple) shows a similar curve shape but converges to a higher plateau, indicating the architecture is capacity-limited rather than under-trained: additional epochs provide diminishing returns.
