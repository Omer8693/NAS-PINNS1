Level 1

Single-Shot NAS-PINN

A single neural network predicts the full 3-D temperature field T(t, x, y) for A356 aluminium water quenching. Neural Architecture Search selects the optimal number of layers, neurons per layer, and activation function.

Global predictor Bayesian · NSGA-II · NSGA-III Target L2 < 0.10 A356 · Robin BC
Problem Setup

Physical Problem

An A356 aluminium casting (1.3 m × 0.6 m rectangle) is quenched from 540°C in 20°C water for 30 seconds. The PINN is trained to reproduce the FEM temperature field at every spatial point and every time step using only the PDE loss and boundary conditions — no labelled FEM data.

Governing PDE

ρCₚ ∂T/∂t = K (∂²T/∂x² + ∂²T/∂y²)

Boundary Condition (Robin)

−K ∂T/∂n = h (T − Twater)

Analytical Reference

T(t) = 20 + 520 · exp(−1.75×10⁻³ · t)

Takeaways

Key Observations

  • ✓
    Bayesian NAS finds the best architecture TPE surrogate efficiently focuses search on promising regions, reaching L2=0.076 — the only optimiser below the 0.10 target.
  • →
    More parameters ≠ better accuracy at fixed budget NSGA-II found 47k params (smaller than Bayesian's 93k) but L2=0.252 — because architecture shape (5 deep layers) matters more than raw count.
  • →
    relu outperforms tanh here Bayesian converged to relu activation. NSGA-II and NSGA-III both selected tanh and performed worse — likely due to gradient saturation in deep networks.
  • →
    NSGA-II and NSGA-III need more training epochs Their architectures are capable but under-trained at 500 NAS epochs. Level 5 extended training closes this gap dramatically.
