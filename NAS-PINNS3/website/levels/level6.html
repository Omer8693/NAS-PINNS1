Level 6

Poisson Fine-Tuning

An auxiliary Poisson PDE loss is added to the quenching PINN training. The steady-state Poisson equation shares the same spatial operators as the heat equation. Adding it as a secondary objective acts as a regulariser, improving generalisation slightly.

Auxiliary PDE loss Poisson regularisation Bayesian gains +17%
Concept

Auxiliary Poisson Loss

The heat equation at steady state reduces to the Poisson equation. By training the PINN to also satisfy a simple Poisson problem on the same domain, we encourage the network to learn better spatial operators — which in turn helps the time-dependent quenching problem.

Heat equation (primary)

ρCₚ ∂T/∂t = K ∇²T

Poisson equation (auxiliary)

−∇²u = f(x, y) on Ω
u = 0 on ∂Ω

Combined loss

L = L_quench + λ_poisson · L_poisson

Why it helps

  • Poisson shares the Laplacian ∇² with the heat equation
  • Extra constraint prevents the network from over-fitting to early time steps
  • Regularises the spatial frequency response
  • No extra training data needed — Poisson solution is analytical

Limitation

The benefit is marginal (+17% for Bayesian, negligible for others). The quenching and Poisson problems differ enough that the shared spatial structure provides only limited transfer.

Takeaways

Key Observations

  • ✓
    Auxiliary PDE loss is a valid regularisation strategy Bayesian gains +17% improvement (0.037 → 0.031), confirming that sharing spatial operators across PDE families can transfer useful inductive bias.
  • →
    Effect is small and architecture-dependent NSGA-II gains only 4%; NSGA-III slightly worsens. The regularisation benefit does not scale uniformly across all architectures.
  • →
    Poisson and quenching share spatial structure, not temporal The steady-state Poisson problem has no time dimension. The transfer is limited to spatial smoothness — which partly explains the marginal benefit.
  • →
    More targeted multi-task learning could improve results A dynamic weighting of the Poisson loss, or using it only during warm-up, may yield larger benefits — a direction for future work.
