Neural Architecture Search for
FEM Time-Step Acceleration
in Aluminium Casting Simulation

FEM advances the quenching simulation one time step at a time. NAS-optimised PINNs learn to skip multiple FEM steps in a single forward pass — cutting compute time while preserving thermal and distortion accuracy.

Mortensen, Noorsumar, Fjær, Babaei, Drønen (2026) · Int. J. Advanced Manufacturing Technology · DOI: 10.1007/s00170-026-17515-w

Summary

Key Findings

  • ✓
    PINN replaces FEM steps without accuracy loss Bayesian skip=2: MAE 33.2°C vs 43.6°C baseline — 2× fewer FEM calls, better result.
  • ✓
    Architecture size determines skip capability Bayesian 5×151 relu (93k params) converges at skip=4. NSGA-III 3×75 (12k params) fails at skip=2 with 500 epochs.
  • ✓
    Extended training closes the accuracy gap NSGA-II: L2 0.252 → 0.055 (4.5× improvement) with longer cosine LR — now meets target.
  • ✓
    Hybrid FEM+PINN: 80–95% step reduction Residual-based routing (threshold=0.1) automatically calls FEM only when PDE residual is large.
  • ✓
    Epoch count was the bottleneck, not architecture At 2 000 epochs: NSGA-II ratio=0.98× ✓ and NSGA-III ratio=0.99× ✓ — both converge at skip=2.
  • ✓
    NAS generalises across PDE families and geometries Circle L2=0.00014 · L-Shape L2=0.028 · 4 of 4 geometries successfully solved with NAS.
