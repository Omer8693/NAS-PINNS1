Abstract

Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) offer a mesh-free approach to solving partial differential equations governing heat transfer. However, their performance is highly sensitive to architecture design and loss balancing strategy.

This work presents NAS-MCO-PINNs, a framework that automatically discovers optimal PINN architectures using Neural Architecture Search (NAS) while employing Multi-Loss Consistency Optimization (MCO) for adaptive loss weighting. A residual skip operator \(\hat{\theta}(t+s\cdot\Delta t) = \theta_\text{IC} + \tau \cdot f_\theta(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z},\tau,\theta_\text{IC})\) is introduced, allowing the network to predict \(s\) time steps ahead while automatically satisfying the initial condition at \(\tau = 0\).

Applied to A356 aluminum water quenching across four 3D geometries (including a complex L-shaped domain with numerical FD reference), the framework achieves 2.0°C MAE on 2D problems (94% improvement over L2 baseline) and reduces finite element computation by up to 81% with skip=6.

Key Contributions

NAS Search

Automated architecture discovery using Bayesian Optimization, NSGA-II, and NSGA-III across depth, width, and activation functions.

MCO Weighting

Adaptive loss weights \(w_i = 1/\sigma_i^2\) that automatically balance physics, boundary, and initial condition losses during training.

Skip Operator

Residual skip network predicts multiple time steps ahead, reducing FEM computation by 52% (skip=2) to 81% (skip=6) while maintaining accuracy.

3D Domains

Validated on four 3D geometries: Rectangular Prism (1.3×0.6×0.4m), Cylinder (R=0.25m, H=0.6m), Stacked Cubes, and L-Shape (0.8×0.8×0.4m, numerical FD).

Method Overview

Physics Equations

3D Heat Equation:

$$\rho c_p \frac{\partial T}{\partial t} = \nabla \cdot (k \nabla T)$$

PINN Loss (MCO):

$$\mathcal{L} = w_\text{target}\,\mathcal{L}_\text{target} + 0.05\,w_\text{PDE}\,\mathcal{L}_\text{PDE}$$

Skip Operator:

$$\hat{\theta}_{k+s} = \theta_\text{IC} + \tau \cdot f_\theta(\bar{x}, \bar{y}, \bar{z}, \tau, \theta_\text{IC})$$

MCO Weights:

$$w_i = \frac{1}{\sigma_i^2}, \quad \sigma_i \leftarrow \text{running std of } \mathcal{L}_i$$
